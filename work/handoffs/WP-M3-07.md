## What it is

* **Concurrency limit (RUN-040).** A gradient limiter: it compares the recent round-trip time with a no-load baseline learned from **low-load samples only** (a quarter of the limit or less in flight, the higher of the concurrency at admission and at completion), shrinks the limit when
  latency rises above `1.5 x` baseline and grows it by `sqrt(limit)` otherwise; updates are applied at most once per four round trips (>= 100 ms), never shrink a limit nobody is using, and an explicit drop signal (a timeout) cuts it by 10% (once per 100 ms). 256 / 16 / 8,192.
  Requests beyond the ceiling of their class are shed (`GAP_OVERLOADED` + `Retry-After` = twice the recent latency clamped to 100 ms..5 s), never queued: interactive 100%, batch 60%, background 30% of the limit.
* **Rate limits (RUN-050, SEC-050).** A token bucket per `(tenant, principal)` (50 rps, burst 100) and a planned-cost budget (600,000 units/min), exact integer arithmetic (nano-tokens), `Retry-After` is the exact wait; the subject table is bounded (100,000) with LRU eviction.
* **Order**: rate limit, then concurrency permit, then (after planning) cost charge; a refused request consumes nothing downstream.

## Decisions and deviations

* **Baseline design.** A first version used a running average and then a minimum with upward drift; both failed the simulation: an average follows the latency up as queues build (limit grew to 8,192), a drifting minimum lets the latency target creep up under sustained overload (p99 6.7x unloaded).
  The final design learns the baseline from low-load samples (two-window minimum, 150 s windows) and freezes it while the gateway is loaded; **only when the limit has collapsed to its floor and no low-load sample arrived for 60 s** does it drift up (2%/s, never above the observed RTT), so a permanent upstream slowdown cannot pin the limit at the floor.
  Consequence (documented, tested): after a permanent slowdown under sustained saturation the limit leaves the floor but stays low until load subsides enough for low-load samples to re-teach the baseline (<= two windows).
* **Default shares 60/30** (not the first 80/50): with FIFO service behind the gateway, batch at 80% of the limit leaves interactive too few slots; the load test pins the numbers.
* **Streaming (RUN-051).** The permit of an SSE response is held by the body and released by a watchdog after `max_deadline` even when the consumer never reads; the *per-frame stall* of the spec is moot for the current two-frame SSE (the channel never fills) and returns with real subscriptions.
* `WP-M3-07` owns more than the plan said (gateway, pipeline, modules, sse, lib, admission, two test files): the plan entry was amended and the WP ordered after `WP-M1-02`.
* Not wired to `gap.toml` yet: `LimiterConfig`/`RateLimitConfig` are constructed in code with the specified defaults; `[limits]` keys and the `gap-server` wiring (`GatewayAdmission` in `AppState::with_admission_policy`) belong to a follow-up of `WP-M1-12`/`WP-M7`. No Prometheus gauges yet (`LimiterSnapshot` exposes limit, in-flight, admitted and shed counters per class).
